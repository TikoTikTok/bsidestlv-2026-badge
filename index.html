Stage — A Mad Tea Party ● armed

how it works

One wall splits the room and the only way through is the gate, shut until Alice steps on the lever. Every rock starts on the wrong side, so each has to be lined up on the gate's row and shoved through a one-tile corridor — the order is the puzzle. The door opens once all three buttons are held.

The badge's d-pad moves, START resets; the stage last tapped is the one the badge drives. On a local checkout the arrow keys or WASD, Enter and R work too; the published site plays from the badge alone. Solve runs a real search over the stage and plays back what it finds — it is not a recorded answer.

Stage — The Queen's Gauntlet ● armed

how it works

This room runs on a clock. Every tick the cast advances one cell along a fixed loop, so a plan is a schedule: one corridor joins the two halves, the Queen walks it looking three cells ahead, and both rocks plus Alice have to cross it several times over. Standing still is a move — the shortest solution waits five times.

The badge's d-pad moves, Y waits a tick, START restarts; on a local checkout the arrow keys or WASD, space and R do the same. Miss a window and the Queen sees you, which ends the run. Par is 64 ticks; an empty room is 41, so the cast costs 23.

The glitch range

Three more rooms, on their own page, built for the badge's quick glitch layer: a combo the Rabbit chimes faster than a hand can follow, the Queen's guard running a routine whose one check has to be skipped with a pulse on X that lands inside a 20 ms window, and the Caterpillar's door, which gives its password away by how long it takes to say no. Record a take on the badge, speed it up, dial in the offset, fire — and read the measurements off the log.

The firmware that does the recording goes onto the badge from the flash page: put the badge in its bootloader and a Chromium browser writes the image over WebUSB, no tools to install.

The badge is the key: the vault page reads the stage key a badge carries and writes one at the booth. On the published site the stages play from the badge alone.

Themes and looks

What the site is painted in — the conference's lime on black, the Queen's court, the tea party in daylight — and how it sits on the screen: the classic page, or a phone look that shows one panel at a time. The ◐ chip in the header opens these same cards; ?theme=queen and ?look=scope in the address bar pick too, and the choice is remembered on this device.

8-way walk cycles

24 frames per character — 8 directions × 3 frames (stand, step, step), indexed by tools/build-walk-manifest.mjs. Click a direction, or use the arrow keys / WASD (two keys for a diagonal), to drive the big one.

S stand

Tiles

Cut from references/tiles-reference.png by tools/slice-tile-sheet.py. Floors and walls fill the whole 48×48 cell — they are the grid. Doors, buttons and levers keep their aspect and sit on the cell floor, sharing one scale factor so a button stays smaller than a door.

Items

Cut from references/assets-reference.png by tools/slice-item-sheet.py — one PNG each, 48×48, transparent. They share a single scale factor, so a key stays small next to a boulder. Click a name to copy it.

// characters: assets/characters/<name>/<dir><frame>.png  + <name>.json

// items and tiles: assets/<items|tiles>/<name>.png + the folder's .json
